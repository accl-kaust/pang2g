\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented G2G, the first system that computes base-pair-resolution graph-to-graph alignments of pangenome variation graphs---a capability proposed by the PGVF format~\cite{pgvfspec} but previously unimplemented---with two modes at different stages of validation. The \emph{reference-anchored diff}, the primary validated result, is single-threaded and exact under identical variant representation (the precondition holds for same-tool construction but not in general); it scales near-linearly to an 8-Gbp pair on one core with 100\% precision and recall on planted variants satisfying this precondition, honestly reported within its stated construction. A \emph{parallel snarl-pairing aligner} is designed for the general case (nested snarls, arbitrary graph pairs) and implements the full systems machinery---minimiser anchor index, LAPJV matching, centralised priority-dispatch scheduler, vEB layout---but is validated only on small-scale, depth-1, short-variant workloads ($50.1\times$ at 64 threads, $12.7\times$ vs.\ linearise-and-map at $h{=}200$); deployment-scale validation on nested snarls and SV-rich inputs is future work. A preliminary biological run confirms the pipeline produces a delta on real data, but cannot test either SV detection or population specificity (SV-depleted input, uncontrolled null, unequal panels, flat $F_{ST}$); a controlled biological validation is also future work. Together, the two modes show that graph-to-graph alignment is a viable primitive for the pangenome era; the reference-anchored diff demonstrates this concretely, and the parallel engine is a first step toward the harder general case.
