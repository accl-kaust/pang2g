\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented G2G, the first system that computes base-pair-resolution graph-to-graph alignments of pangenome variation graphs---a capability proposed by the PGVF format~\cite{pgvfspec} but previously unimplemented---with two complementary modes. The \emph{parallel snarl-pairing aligner}, the primary contribution, exploits the snarl-tree structure of variation graphs to decompose the alignment into a parallel sweep of independent bipartite-matching subproblems (each solved optimally, though snarl pairing is greedy), made fast by a minimiser anchor index, exact LAPJV matching, and a centralised priority-dispatch runtime, each motivated by direct profiling (a cache-oblivious snarl-tree layout is implemented for nested snarls but unexercised on the depth-1 workloads evaluated). Its per-snarl DP scales to $52.6\times$ on 128 threads, and against a linearise-and-map baseline G2G pulls ahead from $h{\approx}10$ haplotypes to $12.7\times$ at $h{=}200$; ablations confirm every design choice earns its place. The complementary \emph{reference-anchored diff}, single-threaded and exact under identical variant representation (the precondition holds for same-tool construction but not in general), scales near-linearly to an 8-Gbp pair on one core with 100\% precision and recall on planted variants satisfying this precondition; on real African vs.\ East Asian pangenomes its small-variant delta shows elevated density near gnomAD-SV breakpoints and ClinVar pathogenic sites ($p<10^{-3}$ vs.\ a uniform null), though the uniform null does not control for regional mutation-rate covariation, so the enrichment may reflect baseline genomic correlation rather than a G2G-specific signal (a region-matched null is needed to distinguish the two). Together, the two modes show that graph-to-graph alignment is a viable primitive for the pangenome era, and G2G is a first step toward it.
