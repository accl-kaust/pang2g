\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented PanG2G, the first system that computes base-pair-resolution graph-to-graph alignments of pangenome variation graphs. The reference-anchored diff, the primary validated result, is exact under identical variant representation and scales near-linearly to an 8-Gbp pair on one core. The parallel snarl-pairing aligner carries the full systems specification (minimizer index, LAPJV matching, priority-dispatch scheduler, vEB layout), is validated on depth-1 workloads up to $10^5$ snarls ($50.1\times$ DP-phase speedup at 64 cores, $51\times$ faster than linearize-and-map at $h{=}200$), and its anchor-interval pairing removes the positional drift of boundary-key pairing. A biological run confirms the delta is meaningful on real data. Graph-to-graph alignment is thus a viable pangenome-era primitive: the reference-anchored diff demonstrates it concretely, the parallel engine takes a first step toward the general case.

All real inputs are public: the 1000 Genomes phase-3 panel (release 20130502), GRCh37, gnomAD-SV v2.1 sites \cite{collins2017svfst}, the ClinVar GRCh37 VCF (weekly release of 28 September 2026) \cite{landrum2018clinvar} and the PGGB HLA-DRB1 test haplotypes; the repository's scripts download them and rerun every experiment and figure reported here.
