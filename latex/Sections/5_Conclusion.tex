\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented G2G, the first system for base-pair-resolution graph-to-graph alignment of pangenome variation graphs. By exploiting the
snarl-tree structure of variation graphs, G2G turns an NP-hard problem into a parallel sweep of small, independent bipartite-matching subproblems, made fast on a single machine by a cache-oblivious snarl-tree layout, a minimiser anchor index, exact LAPJV matching, and a work-stealing runtime, each motivated by direct profiling of the workload. G2G computes, for the first time, the population-specific delta graph between two pangenomes directly, without routing through a linear reference. The reference-anchored diff is exact and near-linear to an 8-Gbp pair on one core; the work-stealing engine scales the per-snarl DP to $52.6\times$ on 128 threads; against a linearise-and-map baseline
G2G pulls ahead from $h{\approx}10$ haplotypes to $12.7\times$ at $h{=}200$. Ablations confirm every design choice earns its place; and on real African vs. East Asian pangenomes the delta graph is significantly enriched near structural variants and pathogenic sites ($p<10^{-3}$). We believe graph-to-graph alignment is a foundational primitive for the pangenome era, and G2G is a first step toward it.
