\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented G2G, the first system for base-pair-resolution graph-to-graph alignment of pangenome variation graphs, with two complementary modes. The \emph{parallel snarl-pairing aligner}, the primary contribution, exploits the snarl-tree structure of variation graphs to turn an NP-hard problem into a parallel sweep of independent bipartite-matching subproblems, made fast by a cache-oblivious layout, a minimiser anchor index, exact LAPJV matching, and a work-stealing runtime, each motivated by direct profiling. Its per-snarl DP scales to $52.6\times$ on 128 threads, and against a linearise-and-map baseline G2G pulls ahead from $h{\approx}10$ haplotypes to $12.7\times$ at $h{=}200$; ablations confirm every design choice earns its place. The complementary \emph{reference-anchored diff}, single-threaded but exact, scales near-linearly to an 8-Gbp pair on one core with 100\% precision and recall; on real African vs.\ East Asian pangenomes its delta is significantly enriched near structural variants and pathogenic sites ($p<10^{-3}$). Together, the two modes show that graph-to-graph alignment is a viable primitive for the pangenome era, and G2G is a first step toward it.
