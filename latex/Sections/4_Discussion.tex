\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Every major decision in PanG2G traces to a measured property of the workload. Snarl-tree factorization is what makes an NP-hard problem tractable. LAPJV replaces brute force matching because matching cost explodes with $k$ (Finding~1); centralized priority dispatch replaces static partitioning because per-snarl work is heavy-tailed and scale-invariant (Finding~2) an argument the static-partition ablation now confirms directly (Fig. \ref{fig:ablation}); and the cache-oblivious layout plus flat sequence storage address the memory wall (Finding~3). Two stages were originally quadratic: anchor detection and snarl decomposition. The minimizer index removes the first; the windowed sink search of \S\ref{sec:algo} removes the second, leaving an end-to-end pipeline that is near-linear in graph size (Fig. \ref{fig:refdiffscaling}).

\subsection{Limiations}
The decomposer is DAG-only: although an optional vg-snarl backend can supply snarl boundaries, the allele enumeration and DP assume acyclicity, so tandem repeats (cycles) are out of scope and full cyclic support is future work. Snarl pairing is greedy rather than globally optimal; its ${\sim}20\%$ error at 5-50 snarls may compound at deployment scale, so interval-restricted, order-preserving pairing between anchors, and ultimately a full tree-matching pairing, is the most important next step. The centralized queue caps scaling beyond 64 cores; a hybrid LPT/work-stealing runtime would lift it. PanG2G is single-machine; the $K>2$ multi-graph alignment problem and a distributed engine are natural extensions, as is GPU acceleration of the per-snarl matching, which is embarrassingly parallel across snarls.
