\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Every major decision in PanG2G traces to a measured property of the workload. Snarl-tree factorization makes an NP-hard problem tractable; LAPJV replaces brute-force matching because matching cost explodes with $k$ (Finding~1); centralized priority dispatch replaces static partitioning because per-snarl work is heavy-tailed and scale-invariant (Finding~2), which the static-partition ablation confirms directly (Fig. \ref{fig:ablation}); and the cache-oblivious layout plus flat sequence storage address the memory wall (Finding~3). The minimizer index and the superbubble scan remove the two originally quadratic stages, leaving an end-to-end pipeline that is near-linear in graph size (Fig. \ref{fig:refdiffscaling}).

\subsection{Limitations}
The decomposer is DAG-only: although the vg-snarl backend can supply boundaries, allele enumeration and the DP assume acyclicity, so tandem repeats (cycles) are out of scope. The evaluation is depth-1 and same-builder only; nested Minigraph-Cactus graphs and cross-builder comparison, which needs variant normalization beyond left-alignment, remain to be tested, and the HLA experiment shows that even same-builder graphs diffed by coordinate inherit the caller's indel placement. Snarl pairing is a heuristic: the boundary-key rule drifts once keys repeat and snarls are private to one graph, the anchor-interval rule removes that drift on our planted graphs but is optimal only within an interval, and a full tree-matching pairing remains the principled end point. Scaling beyond 64 cores is untested. PanG2G is single-machine; the $K>2$ multi-graph problem, a distributed engine and GPU acceleration of the per-snarl matching, which is embarrassingly parallel across snarls, are natural extensions.
